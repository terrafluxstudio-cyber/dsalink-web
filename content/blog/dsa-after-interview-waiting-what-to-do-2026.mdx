The interview, audition or trial is behind you. Your child has told you their version of how it went, and now there is an open stretch of weeks before anything is decided. We've written separately about [what is actually coming and when the results land](/blog/dsa-interview-over-when-do-results-come-2026) — the short version is that outcomes arrive **by Friday, 28 August 2026**, silence before then means nothing, and schools contact you directly.

This piece is about the other half of the wait: the *doing*. Because for a lot of careful parents, the hardest thing about this stage is the pull to keep acting — to send one more thing, ask one more question, tidy up one more detail. Most of those instincts feel responsible and change nothing. A couple are worth understanding before you act on them.

## Don't chase the school for a result

The most common urge is to email or call the school to ask where things stand, or to read meaning into how long it's taking. It won't move anything. MOE publishes **no schedule** for how schools run their timing, and each school notifies families directly when it has decided — some earlier, some right up against the 28 August deadline. A quiet inbox is a school still working through its process, not a verdict you can hurry along by asking. The one date that carries information is the 28th; until then there is genuinely nothing to read in the silence.

## Don't try to "top up" the application

This is the one that trips families up, so it's worth being precise. A parent will sometimes think: my child's mid-year or [prelim](/dsa-results) results came out stronger than the grades we submitted — can I send them to the school now to strengthen the case?

The honest answer is no, and for two separate reasons. First, the **2026 application window closed on 2 June** — the submission stage is long over, and there is no official channel to add or update materials after it. Second, the timing simply doesn't line up: prelim results land in late August or September, by which point DSA outcomes are already being released. There is **no MOE mechanism** for supplementing an application with later results, and anything you've heard along those lines is folklore, not process. It's a natural instinct — but chasing it only adds anxiety, and there's nothing on the other end to receive it.

## A thank-you note is fine — just hold the right expectation

Sending a short, courteous note to a coach or teacher who ran the session is a perfectly reasonable thing to do, and no one will hold it against you. Treat it as ordinary good manners, not as a lever. It won't shift a selection decision one way or the other, so send it if it feels right to your family and skip it if it doesn't — and either way, let it be the end of the contact, not the start of a back-and-forth.

## Put the energy where it still counts: PSLE

Here is the part that actually rewards effort. Whatever the DSA result turns out to be, **PSLE remains the deciding exam.** A Confirmed Offer is conditional on your child meeting the school's minimum [Posting Group](/dsa-results) at PSLE; and if no DSA place comes through, PSLE and S1 Posting are the main route to secondary school, with S1 Appeal available on top. Either way the same months of preparation are the thing that matters most — and unlike the DSA wait, it is entirely within your child's control.

So the most useful reframe for this stretch is almost the opposite of the instinct: the productive move is to *stop* managing the DSA outcome, which is now out of your hands, and quietly return the household's attention to the exam that isn't. Keep the routine steady, keep the pressure low, and let 28 August come when it comes.

When it does, the result will take one of three shapes — a confirmed offer, a wait list place, or no offer this round — and each carries its own next decision. We walk through all three, and the MOE rules behind them, in the [DSA-Sec results guide](/dsa-results).
