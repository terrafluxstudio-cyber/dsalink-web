Once the interview or trial is behind you, the urge is to keep doing something. Send one more thing, ask one more question, fix one more detail. This piece is an honest look at that stretch: what is still worth your effort, what a school might yet ask of you, and how much of it is now simply out of your hands.

First, correct a common assumption: it isn't necessarily over. For most families the interviews and trials wind down through August, but not for everyone. Some schools hold a second round. Others come back to shortlisted families for more before they decide. So before you file the whole thing under "waiting," work out which of these you're in. The two ask different things of you.

## If a school is still deciding, it may ask for more

If your child is still in a school's process, watch your inbox. Schools sometimes ask shortlisted families for extra material: a short write-up or personal statement, a CCA or VIA record. The turnaround can be tight. If a request comes, answer it promptly and plainly, as your child actually is, not as a marketing team would. A quick true reply beats a polished one.

Be clear on one thing, because it's tempting to hope otherwise: what schools want at this stage is supporting material, not new grades. There is no official way to update the exam results you already submitted. MOE's application window closed on 2 June, so a stronger later result isn't something to chase from the primary school or send in unasked. If a school wants more, it will ask. Until it does, there's nothing you're meant to be sending.

## What the school is weighing, and why little of it is now in your hands

It helps to know what a school is really weighing, because it cuts the wait down to size. MOE says schools look at a child's talents and achievements, personal qualities and academic suitability, drawn from the whole school track record and the application, not one afternoon's performance. Much of that was built over years and settled long before the interview. It sits with the school and the record, not with you.

Sit with that, because it changes what the helplessness means. There is little to do right now not because you've missed something, but because the heart of the decision was never in these last few weeks. You can't reshape it from the outside, and that isn't a loss. It means you're allowed to stop pushing.

## Don't chase a school that has already finished

If a school has finished and you're just waiting on the result, the hardest thing is to leave it alone. It is also the right thing. Emailing to ask where things stand won't move anything, and reading meaning into how long it takes will only cost you sleep. Schools notify families directly, on their own timing, by Friday, 28 August 2026. A quiet inbox before then is a school still working, not a verdict you can hurry.

We wrote about [why that silence carries no signal](/blog/dsa-interview-over-when-do-results-come-2026) earlier this month. The short version: the only date that means anything is the 28th, and another family hearing sooner tells you nothing about your own result.

## Where your effort still counts: PSLE

This is the part that repays the effort. Whatever the DSA outcome, PSLE is still the exam that decides things. A Confirmed Offer only holds if your child meets the school's minimum [Posting Group](/dsa-results) at PSLE. And if no DSA place comes, PSLE and S1 Posting are the main route into secondary school, with S1 Appeal on top of that. Unlike the DSA wait, which is now mostly out of your hands, the exam is fully in your child's.

So the most useful thing you can do now runs against the instinct: stop managing the outcome you can't change, and turn the household's attention back to the one you can. Keep the routine steady, keep the pressure low, and let 28 August come when it comes.

When it comes, it will be one of three things: a confirmed offer, a wait list place, or no offer this round. Each carries its own next decision, and we walk through all three, with the MOE rules behind them, in the [DSA-Sec results guide](/dsa-results).
