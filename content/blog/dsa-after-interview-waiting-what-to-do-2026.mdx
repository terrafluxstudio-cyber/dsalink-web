Once the interview or trial is behind you, the instinct of a careful parent is to keep doing something — to send one more thing, ask one more question, tidy one more detail. This piece is an honest map of that stretch: what is still worth your effort, what a school might yet ask of you, and the larger part that is now, quietly, out of your hands.

First, a correction to a common assumption: it is not necessarily over. For most families the selection activities wind down through August — but not for everyone. Some schools run a **second round** of interviews or trials, and some come back to shortlisted families for more before they decide. So before you file the whole thing under "waiting," it's worth knowing which of these you're actually in, because the two situations ask different things of you.

## If a school is still deciding, it may ask for more

If your child is still inside a school's process, keep an eye on your inbox. Schools can come back to shortlisted families for **supplementary material** — a short write-up or personal statement, a CCA or VIA record — and these requests often carry a tight turnaround. If one comes, a prompt and honest reply matters more than a polished one: answer as your child actually is, not as a marketing team would.

One thing to be clear on, because it is easy to hope otherwise: what schools ask for at this stage is *supporting material, not updated grades*. There is no official channel to revise the exam results you already submitted — MOE's application window closed on 2 June — so a stronger later result is not something to chase down from the primary school or send in unasked. If a school wants anything more from you, it will tell you; until it does, there is nothing you're supposed to be sending.

## What the school is weighing — and why so little of it is now in your hands

It helps to know what a school is actually assessing, because it puts the wait back in proportion. MOE is clear that schools consider a child's **talents and achievements, personal qualities and academic suitability** — drawn from the whole *school track record* and the application, not a single afternoon's performance. Much of that picture was built up over years and was set long before the interview, and it sits with the school and the record itself.

That is worth sitting with, because it reframes the helplessness. The reason there is so little to *do* right now is not that you've missed something — it's that the substance of the decision was never in the last few weeks to begin with. It is not something a parent can reshape from the outside now, and that isn't a loss. It's permission to stop trying.

## Don't chase a school that has already finished

If a school's process is done and you're simply waiting on the outcome, the hardest thing is to leave it alone — and it is also the right thing. Emailing to ask where things stand won't move anything, and reading meaning into how long it's taking will only cost you sleep. Schools notify families directly, on their own timing, by the deadline of **Friday, 28 August 2026**. A quiet inbox before then is a school still working through its process, not a verdict you can hurry.

We wrote about [why the silence carries no signal](/blog/dsa-interview-over-when-do-results-come-2026) as August began — the short version is that the only date carrying information is the 28th, and someone else hearing earlier tells you nothing about your own result.

## The one place your effort still counts — PSLE

Here is where the energy actually pays off. Whatever the DSA outcome turns out to be, **PSLE remains the deciding exam.** A Confirmed Offer is conditional on your child meeting the school's minimum [Posting Group](/dsa-results) at PSLE; and if no DSA place comes through, PSLE and S1 Posting are the main route to secondary school, with the S1 Appeal process available on top. Unlike the DSA wait — now largely out of your hands — the exam is entirely within your child's control.

So the most useful move of this whole stretch is almost counterintuitive: *stop* managing the outcome you can no longer change, and quietly return the household's attention to the one you can. Keep the routine steady, keep the pressure low, and let 28 August arrive when it arrives.

When it does, the result will take one of three shapes — a confirmed offer, a wait list place, or no offer this round — and each carries its own next decision. We walk through all three, and the MOE rules behind them, in the [DSA-Sec results guide](/dsa-results).
