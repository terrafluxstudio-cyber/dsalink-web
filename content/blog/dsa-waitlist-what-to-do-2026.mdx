The email arrives, you read it twice, and it doesn't say yes and it doesn't say no. Your child is on the school's **Wait List**. After the months of preparation and the long August of waiting, a "maybe" can feel like the cruellest outcome of all — the door neither opened nor closed.

So before anything else, the two things worth knowing on the day it lands:

- **A Wait List is not a rejection.** It means the school wanted your child and ran out of confirmed places for now. At a popular talent programme the seats are few and the strong candidates many, so a Wait List puts your child just past the line the school could confirm — not short of it. In MOE's own words, your child "may be given a reserved place in the school if other students with confirmed offers did not take up the offer."
- **The odds are better than the word suggests.** MOE's published figure: "in previous years, about half of the students on the schools' wait lists were eventually given a place."

Half is not a long shot. But whether your family is in that half depends partly on something you control — and it's the part most parents, acting on an understandable fear, get exactly backwards.

## What a Wait List actually is

Every DSA-Sec school works through its intake in a fixed order. Students holding a **Confirmed Offer** are placed first — a Confirmed Offer is a *reserved place*, and as long as the child lists that school and meets its minimum PSLE [Posting Group](/dsa-results), the place is theirs. Only once all of a school's confirmed students are settled, and only if seats remain, does the school turn to its Wait List.

That order has one plain consequence: a Wait List place converts when confirmed-offer holders elsewhere decline or don't take up their spots, freeing seats. It is movement you can't see and can't hurry. What you *can* do is make sure that if a seat opens, your child is actually in line to receive it — which brings us to the one real decision.

## The ordering decision most parents get wrong

Here is where families quietly cost themselves the school they wanted.

When preferences open — for 2026, from **9am on Monday, 19 October to 4:30pm on Friday, 23 October** — a child with a Confirmed Offer and a Wait List place can list up to three schools, *in order*. Say your child holds a Wait List place at School A (the one you really wanted) and a Confirmed Offer at School B (a good, safe result). The instinct of a careful parent is to protect the sure thing: put B first, so you don't somehow lose it.

That instinct is the mistake. Ordering the schools is not a gamble between them, because you are not trading one for the other. MOE's guidance is explicit — you can put either option:

- **List A (Wait List) first, B (Confirmed) second.** If a seat opens at A, your child takes it. If no seat opens at A, your child *still* goes to B — because the Confirmed Offer is held regardless. There is no version of this where you lose B by trying for A.
- **List B (Confirmed) first.** Your child goes straight to B, and the Wait List at A can never come through — because confirmed placements are worked out before wait lists are ever reached. You have quietly closed the door you were hoping would open.

Read those two again, because the asymmetry is the entire point. Putting the Wait List school **ahead of** the Confirmed Offer has **no downside** — you keep the safe school if the wait doesn't move. Putting it **behind** the Confirmed Offer guarantees it will never come through.

One clarification that matters: the rule is not "the Wait List school must be your first choice." It is that the Wait List school must sit **above** any Confirmed Offer you're also listing. A family with only a Wait List place and no Confirmed Offer can rank it second or third and still be considered. The trap is specifically ranking a Wait List school *below* a Confirmed Offer — from there, it is unreachable.

And if two children are both wait-listed at the same school and both rank it first? Then the school's own Wait List order decides who is offered a freed seat — which is not something either family can influence now. Your job is only to make sure your ordering doesn't take your child out of the running before that stage is even reached.

## The odds, honestly

"About half" is a real, MOE-published figure. Don't inflate it into a promise, and don't talk yourself out of it either.

It makes a Wait List a live chance — but not a reserved place. Half the wait-listed families are eventually placed; half are not. So work both sides: order your preferences so the wait can convert *if* it moves, and make sure the fallback you settle on — your Confirmed Offer, or PSLE and [S1 Posting](/dsa-results) — is one you'd genuinely accept. Be ready for either.

## When will you actually know?

This is the hardest part to sit with: there is no separate Wait List result day, and no running update as places shift. A Wait List is not resolved on its own clock — it is settled as part of the wider placement process. Seats open when confirmed-offer holders elsewhere decline, and where your child finally lands is worked out through the October preference exercise and confirmed when DSA placements are released with the PSLE results in November.

So between now and October there is nothing to refresh and no call coming. The school won't phone to say the queue moved — and calling *them* to reaffirm your interest won't move it either, because placement is reconciled centrally, not school by school on request. If a Wait List converts, it shows up as a placement, not a phone call. The silence isn't a bad sign — it's just how the process works.

There is one kind of news that reaches you even when the official channels stay silent: the circle. In a small talent community, parents often know within days whose child holds a Confirmed Offer and whose is on a Wait List. It arrives sounding like hard information — and the risk is letting it settle a decision that isn't due yet. Hearing that a school's places are "all taken" can tempt you to write off your Wait List and rank the safe offer first, closing the door yourself. But what travels through the circle in August is a snapshot, not the final count: nothing is settled until the October ranking and the November placement, and seats still come free when confirmed-offer holders don't take up their places. The comparison is real, and it stings — but it isn't the thing your ranking should answer to.

## What to do now

The result has landed, but the decisions don't all happen today. Between now and the October preference window, there is very little to *do* and a few things to get clear on:

- **Nothing forces a choice in August.** It can feel as though holding a Confirmed Offer means committing to it now and letting the Wait List go — but the two are only reconciled later, on the October preference form. A Confirmed Offer that becomes your final placement is binding: a child placed that way cannot then submit choices during S1 Posting, and cannot transfer after PSLE results. That is exactly why you don't want to foreclose the Wait List prematurely, out of an urgency that isn't real. There is no clock making you choose between them today.
- **Decide, as a family, how much you actually want the Wait List school** — because the ordering only matters if that school is the one you'd truly choose over the confirmed one. If on reflection the Confirmed Offer is the school you'd be happiest with, list it first and let the Wait List go with a clear conscience.
- **Keep PSLE at the centre.** Every DSA outcome still runs through PSLE in the end — a Confirmed Offer is conditional on meeting the school's Posting Group, and if no DSA place comes through, PSLE and S1 Posting are the main doors, all still open in November.

So the one step that actually matters is small, and easily missed: put the school you're waiting for **above** any offer you already hold. Do that, and you've given the "maybe" every chance to become a yes — at no cost to the "yes" you already have.
