By **Friday, 28 August 2026**, the schools will have told you. Not MOE, not a portal — each school, on its own, letting you know whether your child has a **Confirmed Offer**, a place on the **Wait List**, a **Counter-Offer**, or an **Unsuccessful** outcome. Some schools send word earlier in the week; the 28th is the outside edge.

On results day, the hard part isn't reading the email. It's what you do next, and in more than one of these outcomes the instinctive move is the wrong one. What follows is a play for each result you might be holding, the MOE rule that governs it, and the part parents most often skip: how to steady your child while you work the decision.

## If you're holding more than one Confirmed Offer

A strong candidate can collect two or even three Confirmed Offers. It feels like winning — and then you remember you can keep exactly one. MOE's rule is plain: **once you accept any one Confirmed Offer, you cannot accept another.** So the decision is real, and it's yours to make well.

The instinct is to rank by prestige and take the "best" name on the list. That's the trap. A DSA place isn't general admission; it's admission into a *talent programme*, and the school with the biggest name isn't automatically the one that will grow your child's real strength. Work the choice on four questions instead:

- **Does this school genuinely resource the talent?** A named CCA is not the same as a serious programme with coaches, facilities, competition exposure, and a track record. The offer that looks lesser on paper may be the one that actually grows your child.
- **Can your child picture themselves *there*,** not on open-house day but on an ordinary Tuesday? Culture and social fit decide whether a talented child thrives or quietly shrinks. A confident performer can still be miserable in a house that runs too intense, or where they don't fit.
- **The commute, honestly.** A one-hour journey feels fine when you're excited. It feels very different at 6pm after training, three or four times a week, for four years. Map the real week, not the brochure.
- **The load after admission.** A DSA talent programme runs on ongoing commitments like rehearsals, trainings and competitions, stacked on top of a demanding academic pace. Ask whether that combination is one your child can sustain for years, not just survive for a term.

The test that cuts through all four: **can you picture your child still saying yes to this school halfway through Secondary 2?** The right DSA school is the one they'd choose again in the middle of the hard part, not the one that looks best the day the offer lands.

## If you have one Confirmed Offer and you're happy with it

The cleanest outcome, and still worth one clear-eyed check, because a Confirmed Offer you accept is **binding**. In MOE's own terms, a DSA student must honour the commitment for the duration of the programme: they **cannot** submit school choices during S1 Posting, and **cannot** transfer to another school after PSLE results are released. There is no published exception.

So before you commit, run that same "Secondary 2" test once. If the answer is yes, accept it and stop shopping. The strategic move here is to *stop*, not to keep wondering whether something better was out there. The offer carries one condition: your child must meet the school's PSLE [Posting Group](/dsa-results). And that is the thread through all of this: PSLE still matters, right to the end.

## If you have a lukewarm offer and a wait list at the school you actually wanted

This is the scenario families get wrong the most. Your child has a **Confirmed Offer** at School B (fine, safe, not the dream) and a **Wait List** at School A, the one you really wanted. A careful parent's instinct is to protect the sure thing: accept B, let A go, don't risk it.

That instinct costs families the school they wanted, because it misreads the mechanics. You are **not** trading one for the other. When the October preference window opens (**9am Mon 19 Oct to 4:30pm Fri 23 Oct 2026**), you rank up to three schools *in order*, and MOE is explicit that you can put either first:

- **Rank A (Wait List) above B (Confirmed).** If a seat opens at A, your child takes it. If none opens, your child *still* goes to B — the Confirmed Offer is held regardless. There is no version of this where you lose B by trying for A.
- **Rank B (Confirmed) above A.** Your child goes to B, and the Wait List at A can never convert — confirmed placements are settled before wait lists are ever reached. You've closed the door yourself.

Putting the Wait List school **above** the Confirmed Offer has **no downside**. Putting it **below** guarantees the wait can never come through. And the odds are better than the word suggests: MOE's published figure is that *in previous years, about half* of wait-listed students were eventually given a place. We wrote the full version of this one decision, including how to handle the noise from your talent circle, in [Your Child Is on a DSA Wait List](/blog/dsa-waitlist-what-to-do-2026).

## If you have a Wait List and nothing else

No Confirmed Offer to fall back on, just the "maybe." The useful thing to know: you don't have to make the Wait List school your first choice to stay in the running. Rank it where it honestly sits in your order of preference, keep the wait alive, and build your fallback around **PSLE and [S1 Posting](/dsa-results)**, both fully open in November. Work both sides at once: give the wait every chance to convert, and make sure the school you'd land in through S1 Posting is one you could accept.

## If nothing came through: all Unsuccessful

This is the outcome that hurts most, and the one most wrapped in myth: **an unsuccessful DSA has zero effect on PSLE posting.** Your child enters the S1 Option Form process normally, on their PSLE Achievement Level total, competing on exactly the same footing as everyone who never applied. Nothing about a DSA rejection follows them into November.

And the doors that remain are real ones:

- **S1 Posting** places your child by PSLE results across the full range of schools, including, often, ones you hadn't seriously considered that turn out to fit.
- **S1 Appeal** is still available at schools with a **sibling or affiliation** link.
- **CCA and open trials** at the posted school are a genuine second route into the very talent your child was building toward.

One pattern worth remembering: a child rejected by every DSA school they applied to can still land, through ordinary posting, at a school with a strong programme in their area, and go on to lead it. A closed DSA door is not a closed door on the talent. It's the honest distinction, and the one your child most needs to hear from you.

## If you got a Counter-Offer, or you're suddenly unsure about DSA at all

A **Counter-Offer** typically comes from a dual-track school offering a place on its O-Level (non-IP) track when you applied for the Integrated Programme. It's a genuine offer with its own trade-offs; we set out how each of the four outcomes binds you in the [DSA-Sec results guide](/dsa-results).

And if the offer in hand has made you realise your family isn't sure about DSA after all, that's allowed too. You can **decline**, and a child who holds no DSA place goes through S1 Posting with complete freedom to choose schools in November. The binding commitment only attaches once you take a place. Turning down a good offer to keep your options open is a legitimate strategy, as long as you and your child make that call together, with clear eyes.

## The part of the strategy parents skip: steadying your child

Most decision guides leave this out. How your child *feels* on results day shapes how well the whole family decides. A distraught child, or a parent radiating disappointment, makes every ranking conversation harder. Handling the emotion well isn't the soft part of results day; it's part of the strategy. Child psychologists are fairly consistent on how to do it:

- **Don't rush to fix it.** Carey Werley, a clinical social worker at the Child Mind Institute, points out that when an outcome can't be changed, the job isn't to repair it but to listen to and acknowledge the disappointment. Reaching straight for "don't worry, it's fine" tells a child their feeling is the problem, and, as the Child Mind Institute warns, brushing off the hurt mostly leaves them feeling more alone with it.
- **Validate before you pivot.** Creative arts therapist Katherine Prudente, also of the Child Mind Institute, frames the first move as naming the feeling — something as simple as *"that's so disappointing, I know how much you wanted this."* A child usually needs to feel understood before they can even hear the alternatives, so the comfort has to come before the plan.
- **Praise the effort, not the result.** Prudente's guidance is to tie a child's worth to character rather than achievement — to be proud of how hard they trained and how much courage it took to try, entirely separate from whether the offer came. This is what protects a child from reading a rejection as a verdict on *them*.
- **Let them help solve it.** Rather than solving everything for your child, Prudente suggests taking a back seat and letting them take the lead. Bringing them into the ranking decision at their level builds the sense that they still have a say in what happens next.
- **Show them you trust them to handle it.** Psychologist Madeline Levine, quoted by the Child Mind Institute, suggests messaging along the lines of *"I know it's really hard, but I think you can handle this."* Said and meant, it does more than reassurance — it hands the child a version of themselves that copes.

None of this means hiding your own disappointment behind a fixed smile; children read that instantly. It means letting yourself feel it, letting them feel theirs, then turning together toward the decision in front of you. A child who feels understood on results day is one who can help you make a good choice by October.

## The one thread through every outcome

Whatever landed in your inbox, two things stay true. **Nothing forces a final choice in August.** The offers, wait lists and rankings are all reconciled later, on the October preference form and confirmed with PSLE results in November. And **PSLE remains the spine of the whole process**: a Confirmed Offer is conditional on meeting the school's Posting Group, a Wait List converts around it, and if no DSA place comes through, PSLE and S1 Posting are the main doors, all of them still open.

So on results day, do less than you think you need to. Read the outcome, steady your child, get clear as a family on what you'd truly choose, and let the real decisions happen on their own timeline.
